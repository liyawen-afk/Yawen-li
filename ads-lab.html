ADS Lab

Adult Day Services Dynamic Capabilities Research Lab

Principal Investigator: Yawen Li, PhD, MSW
School of Social Work, California State University, San Bernardino

This lab examines how adult day health services organizations sense change, adapt, and strengthen their long-term capacity to serve older adults and families across the United States.

About the Project

A national NIH-funded study on organizational adaptation in adult day services.

This study develops and validates measures of dynamic capability—the organizational capacity to sense, seize, and transform in response to environmental change—among adult day health services (ADS) centers nationwide.

Study Period 2025–2029
Current Stage Data Analysis & Scale Development
Project Status Year 2 of 4

Program Objectives

  • Develop a validated instrument measuring dynamic capabilities in ADS organizations
  • Identify internal and external competencies that enable ADS centers to adapt to changing environments
  • Examine how demographic shifts, policy changes, and technological advances affect ADS organizational capacity
  • Generate evidence to support ADS sustainability, quality improvement, and policy advocacy

Research Progress

Stage 1: Qualitative Interviews (Completed)

In-depth interviews with ADS administrators nationwide were completed in Year 1. These interviews identified key dynamic capabilities and competency indicators that now inform scale development.

Stage 2: Survey Development and Validation (In Progress)

Drawing on the Year 1 interview findings, the team is analyzing the qualitative data and developing the initial version of the dynamic capabilities scale. The draft instrument will be refined and validated before national fielding.

Stage 3: National Survey

Administer survey to approximately 200–300 ADS centers across the United States.

Project Update

Year 1 Complete
Qualitative interview recruitment has concluded. We thank the ADS administrators across the country who shared their time and insights. Findings from these interviews are now informing the development of the initial scale.
